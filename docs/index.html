See what your talk evokes.

Every phone in the room becomes a neuron on your slides. And one more neuron listens for applause.

curl -fsSL https://dabane-ghassan.github.io/evoked/install.sh | sh

For the GNOME desktop, 45 or later, on any distro. Paste it in a terminal, then log out and back in once. Version 1.0.1, free and open source.

Click the neurons. Fire six within a blink and they are in sync.

Audience brain

Turn it on and a QR code appears over your slides. People scan it, and each phone is one neuron in a network in the corner of your screen. Nothing to install, no accounts.

  • FreeEvery tap is a spike. The room tries to fire together, and you see synchrony emerge, or not.
  • WaveYou send a wave. Phones light up one after another across the room.
  • VoteTwo choices race to a threshold, pushed by taps. A decision by accumulating evidence.

Room neuron

A leaky integrate-and-fire neuron driven by how loud the room is. It adapts to steady sound, so it fires for what stands out: clapping, laughter, a lively question. At the end you get the raster of your whole talk.

This demo runs in your browser. Nothing is recorded or sent; only loudness is measured.

While you present

Show or hide the join code.

Ctrl Alt R
Room neuron on or off.
Ctrl Alt E
Show the raster of the talk.

Good to know

It draws over anything, fullscreen slides included, and never takes clicks or keys away from your presentation.

Phones reach your laptop through a public relay server, so it works on conference Wi-Fi and on mobile data. Only taps travel through it. You can point it at your own relay in the settings.

There is an app window, a neuron in the top bar, and an evoked command for clickers and scripts.

Made by Ghassan Dabane, under the MIT license. Its sibling is Manicule, a little hand on a stick for pointing at your slides.

An evoked response is what a stimulus brings out in a brain. This one measures a room.
